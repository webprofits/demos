Sound familiar?

The deadline is set, the data is messy and the board wants one clear answer

You've read the TNFD recommendations. You know CSRD asks for a double materiality assessment under ESRS E4. Someone has mentioned SBTN in a meeting.

What you don't have is a defensible position: which framework actually applies to your business, what your supply chain data can and can't support, and what you'd say if an investor asked tomorrow.

  • A 200-page framework explainer that tells you what TNFD is, not what your business should do

Why most nature engagements stall

Frameworks aren't the hard part. Fitting them to your business is

Most consultancies start with the framework and work backwards to you. The result is a report that reads like the guidance and leaves your team no further forward.

Biodiversify starts the other way round. We scope your business, your sites and your supply chain first, then pick the framework elements that fit, then build the disclosure inside your team so the people who have to defend it understand every line.

We call it working with you, not for you. It's why the BAM scoping report became the evidence base their biodiversity managers now use to brief internally.

It depends on where you operate, who's asking and what you're trying to achieve. CSRD is law for in-scope companies and directs a double materiality assessment under ESRS E4. TNFD is the disclosure framework investors increasingly expect. SBTN is how you set science-based targets for nature. Many businesses draw on elements of more than one. Working that out for your business is what the scoping call is for.
