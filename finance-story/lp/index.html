Common questions

What SMSF investors ask before they borrow

How much can my SMSF borrow?

It depends on the lender. Each one looks at the income your fund receives through contributions, rent and other investments, and lends on that. We work out your maximum borrowing amount and your maximum purchase price before you start looking.

Do lenders need cash left in the fund after settlement?

Some do. Some lenders require the fund to hold a percentage of the amount borrowed as cash or shares once the purchase is complete. It's called a liquidity requirement, and it can change how much you can borrow, so we check it first.

Can my SMSF buy the premises my business runs from?

Yes, when it's a commercial property. Your business is able to lease it from your SMSF. You and your relatives can't use a residential property your fund owns.

What will the lender ask my fund for?

Trust deeds, SMSF financials, tax returns and audits, the contract of sale and SMSF bank statements. The lender also asks for proof of the cash your fund contributes at settlement. We work with your accountant and solicitor so every document is up to date.

My SMSF is new and the rollover isn't done. Can I still apply?

Yes. If your funds haven't rolled over yet, you can show that your current super fund holds the required amount. Lenders accept that as proof of the cash contribution.

Is an SMSF loan limited recourse?

Yes. SMSF loans are limited recourse borrowing arrangements, so the lender can claim only the property securing the loan and none of the fund's other assets. The directors of the trustee company also sign a director's guarantee, which lets the lender pursue their personal assets for any shortfall.

Do you advise on whether my SMSF should buy property?

No. That's a question for your accountant or financial adviser, and it's the first step. We arrange the lending and work with them on borrowing limits, repayments and the cash your fund keeps.
